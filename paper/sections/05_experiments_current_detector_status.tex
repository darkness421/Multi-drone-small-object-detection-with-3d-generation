\subsection{Detector Results and Reporting Scope}
\label{sec:detector_results_scope}

We report only paper-eligible detector evidence in the main manuscript. To keep
the paper self-contained and concise, the main detector table includes completed
VisDrone2019-DET validation runs under the normalized 1280-pixel protocol. Rows
from single-seed architecture search, active training queues, 640-pixel
repository sanity checks, and dry-run scripts are excluded from the main table
and are reported in the supplementary material only when they clarify the design
process.

\paragraph{Implementation and evaluation setup.}
Unless otherwise specified, detector experiments use VisDrone2019-DET with
1280-pixel input resolution and the same validation split. The paper-facing
three-seed protocol uses seeds 42, 123, and 2026; each row reports the mean and
standard deviation over the completed seeds. The training server contains an
Intel Xeon Silver 4510 CPU with 12 physical cores, 24 threads, 125 GiB system
memory, and two RTX 5090 GPUs. Parameter counts and GFLOPs are taken from the
corresponding model summaries under the same input protocol. Remaining queue
runs use a shorter early-stopping patience for screening, but final paper rows
are promoted only after the normalized three-seed record is complete.

\paragraph{Main-table rule.}
The main detector comparison contains the selected detector candidate and the
strongest normalized YOLO-family and transformer baselines. Smaller YOLO scale
sweeps, related-work models that currently have external eval-only checkpoints,
NMS sensitivity, Grad-CAM/heat-map analysis, and module-search trails belong in
the supplementary material. This prevents protocol-mismatched rows from
weakening the main claim.

\paragraph{Selected detector candidate.}
The current selected detector candidate is \textbf{SAFR-YOLO}. It keeps a
high-resolution P2/P3/P4 detection path and adds SelfAttnFR refinement with
TinyFReLU; the current implementation label is P2P4-SelfAttnFR. Compared
with YOLOv11l under the same three-seed protocol, it
improves AP from 0.3777 to 0.3822 and AP50 from 0.5981 to 0.6052 while reducing
the parameter count from 25.32M to 20.82M. This is the clean main-paper claim:
better AP/AP50 and fewer parameters than the strongest normalized stock YOLO
baseline. The trade-off cost is higher GFLOPs, so we do not claim a
speed or compute reduction unless later profiling supports it.

The paired-seed test against YOLOv11l gives AP $p=0.0126$ and AP50 $p=0.0223$
with seeds 42, 123, and 2026. Precision, recall, and F1 remain secondary
supporting metrics because their paired significance is weaker at the current
sample size.

\paragraph{Paper-ready detector figures.}
The main paper reserves space for one compact quantitative detector figure and
one qualitative detector figure after final rendering. The compact quantitative
figure visualizes AP/AP50 versus parameter count for
Table~\ref{tab:main_detector_comparison}, while the qualitative figure compares
YOLOv11l and SAFR-YOLO on crowded UAV crops with adjacent objects. Grad-CAM or
heat-map visualizations are referenced in the main text but placed in the
supplementary material unless space remains after the 3D and reasoner sections
are finalized.

\paragraph{Ablation reporting.}
The main paper reports the completed compact final ablation in
Table~\ref{tab:main_detector_ablation}. The table isolates the contribution of
the high-resolution P2/P3/P4 heads, TinyFReLU, and SelfAttn refinement before
the full SelfAttnFR detector. The final detector gives the strongest AP, AP50,
and F1 among these interpretable variants. Single-seed search candidates such as
DCT, wavelet, SE, DynFreq, CBAM, and other module probes are kept in the
supplementary material as design evidence unless they become part of the
selected detector.

\paragraph{Excluded rows.}
CSFPR-RTDETR and LEAF-YOLO are currently useful related-work checks. We have
external checkpoint evaluation rows for them, including 1280-pixel evaluations,
but they are still eval-only rows rather than our normalized three-seed training
protocol. We therefore report them in a separate supplementary related-work
table with an explicit protocol note. The main table remains limited to
protocol-clean trained rows and the selected detector. Likewise, YOLO
nano/small/medium scale sweeps are important for reviewer coverage, but they are
better placed in the supplementary material to preserve the main paper page
budget.

Post-processing NMS sweeps are also kept out of the main comparison until they
are confirmed with the selected detector under the same three-seed protocol. If
the overlap-aware NMS result remains single-seed only, we present it as
an explanatory robustness check rather than as the headline detector score.

\paragraph{Related-work coverage rule.}
Every detector discussed in Related Work is accounted for in the
supplementary coverage matrix as measured, pending adapter/code/weights, or
citation-only. This avoids silently dropping prior methods that are discussed in
the text but cannot be fairly reproduced under our current protocol.
