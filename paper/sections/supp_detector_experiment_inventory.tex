\subsection{Supplementary Detector Experiment Inventory}
\label{sec:supp_detector_inventory}

This supplementary section stores detector evidence that does not occupy the
main-paper comparison table. These rows are useful for reviewer transparency, but
they either expand the YOLO scale sweep, use a protocol that differs from the
main 1280-pixel comparison, or come from design-search experiments rather than
the selected detector.

\paragraph{Main/supplementary split.}
The main paper reports the selected detector, representative strong YOLO-family
and transformer baselines, and a compact related-work comparison/status table.
The supplementary material expands those results with the full YOLO-family
scale sweep, detailed related-work coverage labels, ablation details, NMS
sensitivity, heat-map analysis, and input-resolution stress tests. This split
keeps the main paper self-contained while preserving reviewer-visible evidence
for model-scale and protocol coverage.

following supplementary figures unpack the three detector-side components that
are visually compressed in the main paper: the SelfAttnFR refinement block, the
TinyFReLU activation, and overlap-aware post-processing.

\paragraph{Scale-sweep analysis.}
Table~\ref{tab:supp_yolo_scale_sweep} shows that the proposed detector remains
competitive across the large-model regime while using fewer parameters than
several high-capacity YOLO-family baselines. The nano and small models provide
useful efficiency references, but their AP/AP50 drop is large under the
1280-pixel VisDrone protocol. This supports using a mid-sized detector with
high-resolution heads for the cooperative system: the frontend must retain
small-object evidence before the 3D graph and reasoner can use it.

\paragraph{Related-work analysis.}
Table~\ref{tab:supp_related_work_eval_rows} separates measured cited models
from methods that are only used to motivate design choices. Among the measured
rows, UAVDet is the closest reproduced competitor, but it uses substantially
more parameters than SAFR-YOLO. SFFEF-YOLO and BPD-YOLO are also strong,
supporting the paper's emphasis on frequency response and feature refinement;
however, their measured AP/AP50 remains below Ours in the normalized three-seed
setting. Table~\ref{tab:supp_related_work_coverage} documents the remaining
cited methods without turning non-reproduced papers into unfair numerical
baselines.

\paragraph{Ablation-inventory analysis.}
The supplementary ablation inventory preserves the design history behind the
final detector. It shows that several frequency, attention, and NMS variants
were useful for screening but not promoted because they lacked the same
three-seed support or did not improve the final trade-off. This distinction is
important for reproducibility: the main paper reports the compact, confirmed
module combination, while this appendix explains why nearby alternatives were
not selected.

\paragraph{Post-processing sensitivity.}
Overlap-aware NMS is reported here unless a complete three-seed
confirmation is added to the main detector protocol. The current NMS snapshot is
useful because it shows that adjacent-object post-processing can improve AP and
AP50 on a fixed checkpoint, but the paper labels it as an eval-only
robustness analysis.
